The thread narrowed cleanly: the earlier probe (A — pre-open a cell outside the providing body, resolve two different components over it one after another) gives the same wrong answer on commonJS, erlang and beam (not target-specific, so not a lowering bug), is unaffected by async/await, and shares no cause with B-211 (which is commonJS-only).

Resolution: A is correctly rejected by the language, not a bug. Each body opens its own cell (966 (5)) and `use provide` writes that cell, never one the body was merely handed from outside (968 (2)/(3)) — in probe A the provider and reader are siblings under the pre-opened cell, and 812 (1) scopes a body's provides to its own subtree alone. A second probe (B) — keep the scope a `{ scope, key -> ... }` lambda is handed *inside* the providing body, resolve a reader later over that kept scope — correctly reads the provided value on all three targets, confirming 809 (8)'s actual claim holds. A third probe (C) — resolve a child outside the providing body entirely — reads the default too, which decision 908 already rejects by design.

So front 169's render.bp/streaming.bp need probe B's shape (keep the scope inside the providing body), not A's (pre-open externally). I've already resumed that thread with this exact guidance — it's proceeding on render.bp/streaming.bp now. Updated B-212's spec entry with the full finding and closed its boxes.
